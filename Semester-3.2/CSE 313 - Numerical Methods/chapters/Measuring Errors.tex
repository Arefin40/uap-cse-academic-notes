\chapter{Measuring Error}

\section{True Error}

Defined as the difference between the true value in a calculation and the approximate value found using a numerical method etc.

\begin{center}
   True Error $(E_t)$ $=$ True Value $-$ Approximate Value
\end{center}

\subsubsection{Example}
If $f(x) = 7e^{0.5x}$ and $h=0.3$, then find the Relative True Error for the value of $f'(2)$.

\textbf{Solution:}

\begin{minipage}[T]{0.45\textwidth}
   $\begin{aligned}
      \text{True error}
      & = \text{True Value} - \text{Approximate Value} \\
      & = 9.5140 - 10.263 \\
      & = -0.749
   \end{aligned}$

   \vspace{2ex}
   $\begin{aligned}
      \text{Relative True error}
      & = \frac{\text{True error}}{\text{True Value}}\times 100\% \\
      & = \frac{-0.749}{9.5140} \\
      & = -7.8726\%
   \end{aligned}$
\end{minipage}\vrule\hfill
\begin{minipage}[T]{0.25\textwidth}
   For exact value,\\

   $\begin{aligned}
      f'(x) & = 7 \times 0.5 \times e^{0.5x} \\
      f'(2) & = 7 \times 0.5 \times e^{0.5(2)} \\
      & = 3.5e^{0.5(2)} \\
      & = 9.5140
   \end{aligned}$
\end{minipage}
\begin{minipage}[T]{0.25\textwidth}
   For approximate value,\\

   $\begin{aligned}
      f'(2) & = \frac{f(2+0.3) - f(2)}{0.3} \\
      & = \frac{f(2.3) - f(2)}{0.3} \\
      & = \frac{7e^{0.5(2.3)} - 7e^{0.5(2)}}{0.3} \\
      & = \frac{22.107 - 19.028}{0.3} \\
      & = 10.2646
   \end{aligned}$
\end{minipage}


\section{Approximate Error}
Approximate error is defined as the difference between the
present approximation and the previous approximation.

\begin{center}
   Approximate Error $(E_a)$ $=$ Present Approximation $-$ Previous Approximation
\end{center}

\subsubsection{Example}
For $f(x) = 7e^{0.5x}$ find the relative approximation error for in calculating $f'(2)$ using $h=0.3$ and  $h=0.15$.

\begin{minipage}[T]{0.45\textwidth}
   $\begin{aligned}
      \therefore E_a & = 9.8799 - 10.2646 = -0.3847 \\[3ex]
      \therefore \in_a = & \left| \frac{-0.3847}{9.8799} \right| \times 100\% \\
      & = 3.8938\%
   \end{aligned}$
\end{minipage}\vrule\hfill
\begin{minipage}[T]{0.25\textwidth}
   For $h=0.3$,\\

   $\begin{aligned}
      f'(2) & = \frac{f(2+0.3) - f(2)}{0.3} \\
      & = \frac{f(2.3) - f(2)}{0.3} \\
      & = \frac{7e^{0.5(2.3)} - 7e^{0.5(2)}}{0.3} \\
      & = 10.2646
   \end{aligned}$
\end{minipage}
\begin{minipage}[T]{0.25\textwidth}
   For $h=0.15$,\\

   $\begin{aligned}
      f'(2) & = \frac{f(2+0.15) - f(2)}{0.15} \\
      & = \frac{f(2.15) - f(2)}{0.15} \\
      & = \frac{7e^{0.5(2.15)} - 7e^{0.5(2)}}{0.15} \\
      & = 9.8799
   \end{aligned}$
\end{minipage}


\subsubsection{How is Absolute Relative Error used as a
stopping criterion?}
If
$|\in_a| \leq \in_s$ where $\in_s$ is a pre-specified tolerance, then no further iterations are necessary and the process is stopped.

If at least $m$ significant digits are required to be correct in the final answer, then $|\in_a| \leq 0.5 \times 10^{2-m}\%$


\vspace{2ex}
\subsection{Significant Digits}
Significant digits (also known as precision or resolution) of a number in positional notation are digits in the number that are reliable and necessary to indicate the quantity of something.

Significant digits of a number are those that can be used with confidence.

\vspace{2ex}
\begin{minipage}{0.48\textwidth}
\textbf{Rules:}
\begin{itemize}[parsep=4pt]
   \item All non-zero digits are significant
   \item Trapped Zeros are always significant
   \item Leading Zeros are never significant
   \item Trailing Zeros are
   \begin{itemize}
      \item Significant if Decimal Point
      \item Not significant if no Decimal Point
   \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
