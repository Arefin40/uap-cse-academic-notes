\subsubsection{Binomial Distribution}
Number of favorable outcomes: $C(n, k) = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}$

Probability of each outcome: $p^k \times (1-p)^{n-k}$

Probability: $P(X=k) = C(n, k) \times$ Probability of each outcome

Where\\
$n$ = number of trials \\
$k$ = number of successful trials \\
$p$ = probability of success on an individual trial

\vfill\null

\columnbreak
\textbf{Example:} You sell chicken and egg sandwiches. What is the probability of selling 2 chicken sandwiches to the next 3 customers?

We have $n=3$ and $k=2$.

Number of favorable outcomes: $C(3, 2) = \frac{3!}{2!1!} = 3$.\\[0.75em]
Probability of each outcome: $0.5^2 \times 0.5^1 = 0.125$.\\[0.75em]
$\therefore$ Probability: $3 \times 0.125 = 0.375 = 37.5\%$.

\vspace{1em}

\textbf{Example:} You sell chicken and egg sandwiches. 70\% of people choose chicken, the rest choose egg. What is the probability of selling 2 chicken sandwiches to the next 3 customers? (Baised)

We have $n=3$ and $k=2$.

Number of favorable outcomes: $C(3, 2) = \frac{3!}{2!1!} = 3$.\\[0.75em]
Probability of each outcome: $0.7^2 \times 0.3^1 = 0.147$.\\[0.75em]
$\therefore$ Probability: $3 \times 0.147 = 0.441 = 44.1\%$.

\vspace{1em}

\textbf{Example:} A company makes sports bikes. 90\% pass final inspection (and 10\% fail and need to be fixed). What is the expected Mean, Variance and Standard Deviation of the 4 next inspections?

We have $n=4$, and $p=0.9$.

Here random variable, $X:$ number of bikes that pass inspection.

$\therefore P(X=0) = \left(\frac{4!}{0!4!}\right) \times \left(0.9^0 \times 0.1^4\right) = 0.0001$ \\[0.5em]
$\therefore P(X=1) = \left(\frac{4!}{1!3!}\right) \times \left(0.9^1 \times 0.1^3\right) = 0.0036$ \\[0.5em]
$\therefore P(X=2) = \left(\frac{4!}{2!2!}\right) \times \left(0.9^2 \times 0.1^2\right) = 0.0486$ \\[0.5em]
$\therefore P(X=3) = \left(\frac{4!}{3!1!}\right) \times \left(0.9^3 \times 0.1^1\right) = 0.2916$ \\[0.5em]
$\therefore P(X=4) = \left(\frac{4!}{4!0!}\right) \times \left(0.9^4 \times 0.1^0\right) = 0.6561$ \\[0.5em]

Mean, $\mu = n \times p = 4 \times 0.9 = 3.6$ \\[0.75em]
Variance, $\sigma^2 = n \times p \times (1-p) = 4 \times 0.9 \times 0.1 = 0.36$ \\[0.75em]
Standard Deviation, $\sigma = \sqrt{\sigma} = \sqrt{0.36} = 0.6$
